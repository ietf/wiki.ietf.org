# Charter

DNS directorate reviewers assist Area Directors, Working Group chairs, and document authors with documents containing DNS-related content.

More detailed guidance on DNS directorate process can be found at: [process](#process).

WG chairs or responsible ADs may request a DNS directorate review via the draft's Datatracker page. They are encouraged to do so as early in the process as possible to ensure that structural concerns are caught early in the document development.

The DNS directorate secretaries will assign reviews to reviewers, but they are not required to check the list of IETF drafts for DNS-related ones.

The DNS directorate reviews will be sent to the DNS Directorate mailing list (dnsdir@ietf.org), draft authors, WG chairs, and the respective AD. 

The DNS directorate reviewers and secretary are volunteers, and serve at the pleasure of the INT and OPS area ADs.

# Process

Here are our expectations of the process of assignment of review tasks to members of the DNS Directorate.

Sometimes DNS Directorate  members take an extended period of time to respond to assignment requests. This delay creates unnecessary problems. The co-chairs aren't sure if these requests should be given to someone else, and the longer we wait, the less the time for the subsequent review if we need to re-assign it to another member, which is unfair on them.

If a dnsdir member's availability hasn't been updated in the datatracker, nobody knows if an intended stuckee is unavailable and shouldn't get further assignment requests for a while.

Here are our expectations:

a) If you are going to be out of touch for any reason for more than 1 week please enter your absence into the Datatracker and we won't bug you (you can do this for shorter periods also if you want - its up to you).

b) When we assign a draft to you for review we would like to get your acceptance or rejection within 72 hours (i.e. 3 days) (yes, we know that's a whole lot shorter than 1 week, but if we have to reassign this review then this leaves the next reviewer less time to perform the review).

c) If we have to reasssign the review task to someone else because of no response to the original request within this 72 hour period, then we'll record you as being non responsive for that review assignment.

d) If we mark you as non-responsive three or more times in the most recent 12 months, we will ask you if you are still interested in being a reviewer in the DNS Directorate (and nag you on your nag channel if you have provided one to us).

e) If you don't respond to d) in a further 7 days we will assume a "no" to the question and request the AD to take the necessary action.

We will be using the email address you provided in the datatracker. Please monitor it regularly.

If you give us a nag point of contact (slack or something else that we also use) we will use that for last minute nags when you have not responded to an email request within the 72-hour period.

These are not "rules" per se, but they are our expectations, so that we all can manage the DNS DIrectorate review work smoothly and efficiently, with the minimum of friction and surprises as we go.

# Review Template

```

Reviewer: Your Name
Review Result: Ready/Not Ready/Has Nits/Has Issues/Serious Issues

I have reviewed this document as part of the DNS directorate's 
ongoing effort to review all IETF documents being processed by the IESG.  These 
comments were written with the intent of improving the operational aspects of the 
IETF drafts. Comments that are not addressed in IETF Last Call may be included in AD reviews 
during the IESG review.  Document editors and WG chairs should treat these comments 
just like any other Last Call comments. 
```
