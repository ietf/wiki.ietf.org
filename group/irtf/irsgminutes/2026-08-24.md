## Discussion Topics
### IRTF Research Education
Background: proposal for a community activity around curating and organizing educational resources for Internet research and modern Internet technologies. Initial wiki: https://wiki.ietf.org/group/irtf/edu

#### Feedback on the mailing list has been broadly positive. Main points raised:

- Focus on curation and guidance, rather than simply collecting resources: topic overviews, reading/learning paths, course synopses, etc.
- Distinguish research presentations from material suitable for education; useful sources may include RG material, BoF introductions, tech deep dives, tutorials, ANRW/ANRP material, etc.
- Potential IRTF/IETF added value in identifying good implementations, software, simulators, testbeds, and practical exercises suitable for teaching.
- Could be particularly useful for new/emerging topics, where educational material is fragmented or not yet covered well by textbooks.
- Need to clarify target audiences and intended scope (IRTF research topics only, or Internet research and modern IETF technologies more broadly).
- Main challenge: sustainability – avoiding stale or unbalanced collections and assigning responsibility for maintaining topic areas.
- Need incentives for contributors/curators; possibilities include visible curator ownership, citable artifacts, and potentially some form of community/IRTF endorsement.
- Endorsement or “recommended” resources would require selection criteria and lightweight governance for resolving disagreements.
- Question of organizational form: start as a lightweight community activity, or establish more explicit responsibility (potentially eventually an EDURG-like RG). Concern: avoid creating another RG without clear sustained activity and impact.
- Possible use of AI and collaborative tooling to help maintain and update educational resources.

#### Questions for discussion

- Do we agree that this is an activity the IRTF should pursue?
- What should the initial scope and audience be?
- What would constitute useful deliverables beyond the wiki?
- How should curation/maintenance responsibility be organized?
- Should we start as a lightweight community activity and evaluate later, or is more explicit structure useful from the beginning?
- What would be a useful next step / experiment before IETF-127?

There was general support for the idea, tempered by concerns about scope  and long term maintenance of any materials produced. Discussion also touched on making this a working group to create responsibility, with a different mode of operation.

### Networking for AI Research
- Side meeting at IETF-126, good support
- See pages 18 and following at https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/126/materials/slides-126-irtfopen-chair-slides-01.pdf for summary
#### Potential RG topics that were discussed
Motivation: several IETF activities are addressing near-/medium-term protocol issues around AI agents (DAWN, AGENTPROTO/ACP, DMSC, OAuth/WIMSE, etc.). A Net4AI RG would complement these with longer-term and clean-slate research on networking for distributed, multi-stakeholder AI systems, and provide a bridge to the networking/systems research community.

Potential research topics:

- Network architectures for distributed AI
	- Architectural Internet support for autonomous agents and distributed AI ecosystems
	- Internet-native rather than datacenter-only approaches
	- Principled decentralization and multi-stakeholder AI architectures
	- Inter-datacenter and Internet-scale AI communication
	- Transport and communication for AI workloads

- Adaptation of existing Internet transports to AI workloads
	- New transports for distributed training/inference, including multipath, load balancing, RPC efficiency, encryption, and rapidly changing traffic demands
	- Understanding which AI workload requirements require new transport abstractions rather than incremental protocol extensions

- Agent-specific communication
	- Communication abstractions designed for AI agents rather than human-oriented messaging
	- “Beyond anthropomorphic” agent communication
	- Latent-space communication
	- KV-cache-based communication and other model-native representations
	- Longer-term architectural approaches to an “Internet of Agents”

- AI-oriented caching and content distribution
	- KV-cache-centric networking and disaggregated inference
	- Distribution/reuse of inference state across machines, datacenters, and potentially the Internet
	- New caching/content-distribution abstractions for AI workloads
	- Network support for architectures such as Mixture-of-Experts

- Naming, identity, trust, and security for agents
	- Naming and addressing of agents and AI services
	- Cross-domain and decentralized agent identity
	- Capability-based discovery beyond DNS-centric approaches
	- Trust establishment for dynamic, cross-domain AI workloads
	- Attestation and other new security requirements
	- Foundations for trustworthy agent-to-agent communication

- Experimental methodology and research infrastructure
	- Testbeds and simulators for networked/agentic AI systems
	- Common benchmarks for comparing approaches
	- Shared datasets and reproducible experiments
	- Experimental environments that go beyond single-datacenter AI infrastructure

Overall research objective: develop principled networking abstractions and architectures for distributed and agentic AI, especially in areas currently receiving less attention in mainstream AI-systems research: autonomous agents, decentralization, Internet-scale AI architectures, and multi-stakeholder AI ecosystems.
